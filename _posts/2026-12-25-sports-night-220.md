At the time I started this blog, the division of the company I worked for was in the middle of a cost savings kick. The kick was framed as part of a company-wide drive to reach its latest arbitrary financial goal (one billion dollars in revenue), but it also came in the wake of our division's being in the red the previous year, which is why our division in particular had to heed the call of cost savings. Being software engineers, I and my team were tasked with developing software features to enable others to save on costs, and also generally to find ways to pay for ourselves (it would take too long to explain). Around the same time, we were also tasked with more thoroughly documenting our systems that had so far largely been shot from the hip in terms of documentation — another front in what we were told was a necessary piece of increasing our valuation as a division. In the end, that year we had the largest profit margin of all the divisions in our company, completely paying for the losses of the division that had been the darling child division the previous year.

In hindsight, all the signs were obvious, and I ignored them — much like some other people I could name right about now. To see what I mean, let's step through this episode.
