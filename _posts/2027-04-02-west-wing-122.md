### RETURNING Plot Bunny: What Kind of Day Has It Been™
<sup>Previous instance: _Sports Night_ 123</sup>

I suppose we should be expecting this episode title on every season 1 finale, then?

I want to reiterate how utterly insane it was Mr. Sorkin ran two television shows both at the same time for a full year. That insanity lays itself bare in the publicly available draft script for this episode, which not only went through quite a few changes before shoot for a variety of reasons but also displays some rather glaring internal consistency errors that I would say can only be explained by its writer's running on fumes. If you think I'm kidding, you'll just have to see it for yourself:

... Good god, I got depressed just writing that sentence.

As originally written, the town hall rehearsal was directed not by C.J. but instead by... Mandy, with C.J. serving as the stand-in moderator instead of Carol. In-universe, the change makes sense considering we heard last episode how the recent media strategy was specifically formulated by C.J. rather than Mandy; on a meta level, the change serves to foreshadow an inevitable absence from the opening credits next season.

As I write that, it occurs to me that C.J.'s character development through the first season is theoretically the primary reason why Mandy's character ends up getting put on a bus. Mandy's being hired as a media strategist in the first place was arguably another symptom of the White House staff's general distrust in C.J.'s being able to do her job correctly. As C.J. continually proves through the season how wrong they all are not to trust her — culminating in last episode where _her_ media strategy pays off in dividends — she rather inadvertently shows that hiring Mandy was thoroughly unnecessary. Ironically, C.J.'s push-out of Mandy's character does unfortunately come at the cost of further widening the gender gap in our band of heroes — but perhaps we can expect something to make up for that in the coming seasons? _(crosses fingers)_

Most importantly, by the end of this season, we've been given reason to cheer for every member of our band of heroes... except for one, of course. To be honest, it could be argued that Mandy wasn't really a member of our band of heroes in the first place anyway. Certainly there was an _attempt_ to make her one, based on what we saw cut from the pilot script and what was expected of her from the cursed "Enemies" draft script, but that those bits for her were cut were simply the initial signs that her character was not long for this universe. I feel obliged to say I wouldn't call it Moira Kelly's fault, but rather the fault of the character arcs around her, particularly C.J.'s — Mandy's character simply didn't have the room to breathe... and it's honestly just as well for the series, considering how Mandy represented a practical antithesis to the Bartlet administration's do-good strategy. The bus has now departed... courteously.
