Every once in a while, I do a double-take when watching this episode because it doesn't sound right to me that a Court nominee be confirmed by a simple majority. In those times, I then have to remind myself that there's a difference between the actual confirmation vote and the cloture vote which comes before it, the latter of which required a three-fifths majority at the time of this episode. Confirmation votes have always been by a simple majority — in fact, back in 1881 President Garfield's Supreme Court nominee Stanley Matthews was confirmed by a margin of 24-23, the only nominee in history (so far) to be confirmed by a single vote.
