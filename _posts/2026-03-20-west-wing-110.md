Mercifully for us, the draft script published for this episode is _not_ one of the infamous early drafts. As the story goes, Rick Cleveland wrote initial drafts to have Sam be the hero of the homeless veteran, but somewhere along the way the writers' room moved to have the hero (rightfully) changed to Toby. In response, Rick wrote a new draft which for all intents and purposes just changed the names around, thus having Toby act like Sam for the episode — which is to say, like an asshole.

Richard Schiff was, shall we say, not pleased.

Hold on, someone's knocking on my door. I'll be right back.

Huh, I just had a FedEx guy deliver a letter to me directly. I had to sign for it and everything. I haven't had that happen since my last employer sent a letter to tell me that my employee incentive shares were by some technicality as yet unexplained by mere mortals considered worthless and therefore forfeited. Good times... anyway, let's see what the perpetual scab delivered to me.

Oh! Oh my! I've been nominated for Understatement of the Year! I'm so honored! I'd like to thank the Academy — wait, I'm not in the Academy.

All jokes aside, Mr. Schiff was right to be disturbed, and Mr. Sorkin was likewise right to come in and rewrite the episode — and Mr. Sorkin clearly put a lot of effort into the rewrite, because our draft script in question is dated under two weeks before the episode aired. Despite that time crunch, there was still a rather major change between draft script and final cut, which really speaks volumes about how fun it was to produce this show. How much that change affects the tone of the episode is debatable, of course — but we'll get there when we get there. Let's step through the episode.

> SAM: _(V.O.)_ Previously on _The West Wing_...

Really? Sam still got the _Previously On_™? That's some poor planning.

> MANDY: The President will stand next to the tree with the rope line to the left and the carolers will be off to the side.

This line got changed alongside a change in scenery: as written in the draft, this scene took place in the Mural Room rather than the lobby. I state once again, this draft is dated under two weeks before episode air — on-the-fly changes like this had to have been risky.

Like with episode 109 of _Sports Night_, we see Mr. Sorkin at least partly in tune with the pride of the homeless, where they don't want to accept an excess of generosity. That pride of the homeless is also why the change of scenery is apropos as well: the homeless are not always keen on making a homeless shelter their main grounds, instead typically only seeking it out when the weather calls for it. If you think I'm kidding, I actually have some first-hand experience to report.

Back in the Before Times™, I used to walk between my apartment and where I worked, which was a three-ish block walk. In the interest of full disclosure, my hometown is one of those American cities that was particularly affected by the historical practices of redlining, so my walk took me through one of the "yellow" areas of, er, historically mixed reputation, to put it impolitely. The worst that usually ever meant for me at the time, however, was an older Black man standing at a street corner outside an apartment building each morning greeting everyone who walked by with a happily sincere, "Alright, now, how ya' doin?" It became part of my routine to respond politely with a nod and a smile.

One day on my walk back, however, I crossed paths with a homeless man who, now that I think about it, was dressed not unlike the homeless man who goes unnamed in this scene, except he also had a small backpack on him as well. His ask of me once we had each other's attention was where the other homeless people are. I didn't think much of the wording of the question in the moment and started to direct him toward the closest homeless shelter. Before I could finish, however, he interrupted me sharply, "I don't want to go to a shelter." In response to my shock at the interruption, he asked me simply to direct him to somewhere the homeless would hang out during the day. I then directed him to my best guess at the time, which was the bus station four-ish blocks away — at which point he thanked me and started walking in that direction without any further word.

That was the moment my mind was turned on to the pride of the homeless, so I have to wonder if Mr. Graves perhaps had a similar moment at some point to inform his resetting of this scene. It's not like we can pin the change on the location's being easier or cheaper to get permission to film at, because the soup kitchen line represents an additional effort and expense otherwise not present in the scene.
