Once again, _Sports Night_ differentiates itself from Mr. Sorkin's other television shows by not presenting the title of the episode on screen at any point. If it did, viewers certainly would have been confused by the title of the previous episode, "Kafelnikov", considering the name doesn't appear at all in that episode. That over a month passed before the next episode aired certainly wouldn't have helped the matter.

I won't pretend to know for certain why television shows have gaps in their season schedules like that — though I have a strong suspicion there is something of an anti-burnout element to it in many cases (which I also suspect is why TV shows these days opt for ten-or-so episode seasons instead of the standard 20-24 range). In this case, though, the fact that the two episodes on either side of this gap both lack Mr. Sorkin in the writing credits probably contributed to the gap in some capacity.

If I had to guess, at this point Mr. Sorkin was probably starting to experiment with handing off _Sports Night_ to his fellow writers in the future. (He likely also had his time monopolized by having to fix a couple of horrendous scripts for _The West Wing_ around this time as well... but we'll get into that more two entries from now.) With the last episode, Mr. Sorkin still was clearly involved in the process rather quickly, but with this episode, I would posit his presence came in much later. The writers of this episode had their training wheels come off, and it probably took them a while to get their bearings.

If I'm correct, then that extra time certainly paid off, in my opinion. I do want to reserve for a later entry a more thorough discussion on how Mr. Sorkin had every right to trust this writers' room. For now, let's step through the episode.

I should start out this outro by stating this is a solid half-hour of television. With that said, I should state that despite the obvious hand of Aaron Sorkin its in final form — as evidenced by the various Sorkinisms — there is a decidedly un-Sorkinesque quality to the overall skeleton of the episode. That quality is why I feel relatively confident in the idea that Mr. Sorkin trusted his writers' room for this episode more than for the previous episode without his name on it. Aside from a couple of points that could be considered character continuity breaks, those writers justified that trust — and to be fair, it's not like Mr. Sorkin cared much about continuity breaks himself anyway. I once again want to wait until the topic is even more relevant to discuss further, but that trust in the writers' room could have served as a better omen for the show than it ended up being. For now, it's served very well to let Mr. Sorkin focus on a month's worth of _The West Wing_.
