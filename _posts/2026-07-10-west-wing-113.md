A couple of episodes ago, I mentioned how the White House is tasked (rather unfairly) with putting more emphasis on domestic affairs than on foreign affairs in their PR strategy. One of the downsides of that strategy, of course, is that it requires the White House to deal with not-so-ideal domestic scenarios whose outcomes are largely out of its control. This episode of _The West Wing_ puts a focus on that executive idiosyncrasy by highlighting the Bartlet White House's strategy to blunt such bad news: bundle it all together so less can be written about any of it.

We once again must note how this series was written before the ubiquity of Internet news, considering how nonsensical that idea sounds to people today. With that note in mind:

Overall, this episode takes on much the same approach as "The State Dinner" by overloading the episode with plot threads in the service of conveying the utter chaos of your average day at White House. Interestingly, this episode adopts the admittedly risky tactic of telling us up front that the majority of those threads straight up don't really matter. What's left when sweeping that aside is the continuing adventures of the aftermath of the revelation of Leo's past rehab tenure — and on that front, we are given even more reason to cheer for the character of Leo in the form of his trust in his fellow humans. That he goes one for two on that trust's being vindicated probably could have used some more exploration, but as we've heard and will hear again later, things move fast in the White House, so it's hard to linger in this setting. Besides, we still have nine episodes to go in the season, and we have good reason to look forward to every single one.
