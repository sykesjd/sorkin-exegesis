Mr. Sorkin's attempts at non-linear storytelling for the screen up to this point have been of the epistolary or quasi-epistolary format wherein a present-day character relays the events of the past in order. Now that the man had only one television show to write instead of two, he took the plunge into another form of non-linear storytelling: the interweaving of two linear timelines offset by time — a format he once before explored with the stageplay version of _A Few Good Men_, though that format largely disappeared for the movie version.

The risk of taking on such a format in a teleplay setting rather than a stageplay or screenplay setting, of course, is the possibility of introducing continuity errors relative to the episodes around it — and as we all know, Mr. Sorkin famously doesn't seem to care for television continuity that much. How bad is it in this case? One way to find out:

**WARNING: CONTINUITY ERROR DETECTED!!**

President Bartlet has been established to be a former two-term governor prior to becoming president, and it's later established that the second of those two terms came immediately before his presidency. Someone in the script continuity department dropped the ball here!
