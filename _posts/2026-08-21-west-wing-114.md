By his own admission, Martin Sheen himself suggested to Mr. Sorkin that President Bartlet be a Notre Dame alumnus and a devout Catholic. Mr. Sorkin took him up on the implied challenge — and while the backstory proved largely beneficial, I would argue that backstory was leaned into a little too hard for this episode. The discussion brought about may be profound, but its profundity comes at the cost of a questionable treatment of the First Amendment. I suspect many people would disagree, but I'm sure you'll see what I see when we get to a particular moment. For now, let's step through the episode.

> JUSTICE: Assuming arguendo that the district court erred in allowing the jury to consider non-statutory factors that were vague, overbroad, or duplicative in violation of the Eighth Amendment, such error was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt.

I beg your pardon? "Harmless beyond a reasonable doubt"? Wouldn't it have been the PDs' job to establish _harm_ beyond a reasonable doubt rather than the state's charge to prove a lack of harm? _(sigh)_ Try not to think about...
