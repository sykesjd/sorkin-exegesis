The majority of Mr. Sorkin's forays into non-linear storytelling in his works for the screen come in the format of someone in the present time relaying the events of the past to someone else. So far we've seen the epistolary framing flavor of that format with "Dear Louise"; in later entries, we'll enjoy the many times Mr. Sorkin used the deposition room setting to achieve the same end result. This episode takes on what could arguably be considered a precursor to that proclivity: the lecture. Believe you me, some of Mr. Sorkin's deposition scenes come across as lectures in their own right — but here, we have a proper lecture setup.

What the lecture frames, unfortunately, has some problems that we'll have to discuss when we get there... but for now:

> C.J.: _(V.O.)_ Previously on _The West Wing_...

Let's get into the episode.

Well, what is there to say about this episode? This episode once again takes on the strategy of not letting the story get in the way of some good music — and indeed, there are some deliciously quotable lines in this episode. The story itself isn't even all that bad, mind you, if we limit ourselves to what the lecture framing tells of the past, which is at its core another lean into the chaos of the White House — enhanced with some wonderful character work from Allison Janney. Outside that story, however, we have to confront the Mendoza side-quest, which career government lawyers will tell you is a thoroughly questionable subplot. That the audience is just supposed to take the characters' word for it that racial profiling occurred here weakens the thread further — especially considering the _Previously On_™ sequence sets this episode up to be a standalone — thus essentially reducing the impetus for the thread to a quickly exhausted gag on the title of the episode. We once again must close our eyes and listen strictly to the music for an episode of _The West Wing_.
