Up to this point, _The West Wing_ has largely kept the focus of the White House on domestic matters, aside from a strike on Syria — and a State dinner, if you can even count that. It is a perhaps inevitable consequence of how the White House in real life over the decades largely avoids using accomplishments in their foreign affairs as part of their PR strategy to the nation, despite the fact the White House quite literally _is_ the seat of foreign affairs in the federal government (or at least should be, Constitutionally speaking). For this episode, however, we viewers are finally promised a multi-episode arc focusing on foreign affairs, which I suppose is a better-late-than-never promise. How well developed that arc ends up being is a matter of debate, unfortunately — but that's for later entries. For now:

Between draft and shoot, the timeline of events abroad got shortened considerably — in this case, the satellite imagery went from three hours old to twenty minutes old. I did some digging and found Key Hole satellites around this time clocked in somewhere between 14 and 15 revolutions per day, which means it took somewhere between 1.6 and 1.7 hours to go around the earth once. To have imagery be three hours old, therefore, would imply a pass over the region failed to provide updated photos — so I think it's safe to call this change a correction.

> FITZWALLACE: Sir, already intense fighting has occurred between Indian troops and Pakistani border garrisons. The cease-fire line's been breached in two thrusts, with five divisions invading the area north of Kargil, and a two corps-sized force that's crossed west into Azad.

"Cut! The script says 'east', not 'west', John!"

Actually, hold on... this line is just geographically confusing in its entirety. For starters, it's not made entirely clear which army is performing the breaches of the cease-fire line, or even if one army is doing both breaches or if they are split. The "area north of Kargil" bit would be more likely invaded by Pakistan, as India would have to cross the entirety of Jammu and Kashmir to get that far north. The cross into Azad (which is misspelled Azhad in the draft script) is a little more confusing, as Pakistan has largely administered the Azad region of Kashmir uncontested and therefore doesn't have cause to cross east into Azad, but having India cross west into Azad would once again require they cross a large swath of Jammu and Kashmir to get there in the first place. I'm at a loss.

The writing for this episode does have some roughness around the edges, but it is nonetheless a quality episode of television. The progressive building of tension on the matter of international affairs is quite deftly done, and it's paired nicely with the continuing build-up of the domestic affairs crisis surrounding Leo. The issue of the team's apparent lack of confidence in C.J. is also refreshing to see finally verbalized, as unkempt as its conclusion here may be. This episode is, however, largely one that is dependent on the episodes around it to speak for its quality, which means it probably would struggle as a single-episode example for this series. I wouldn't consider that a count against the episode, though, mind you — it's simply important to note the transitional nature of this episode before we get deeper into the season, which we'll find mightily important soon enough.
