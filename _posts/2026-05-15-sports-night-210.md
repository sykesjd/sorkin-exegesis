> NATALIE: According to the dictionary, 'momentarily' means _both_ 'for a moment' _and_ 'in a moment'.

Nope — Natalie hasn't learned the basic truth of dictionaries: that dictionary publishers conform to popular usage of words even if those usages are semantically incorrect. Don't get me wrong, I'm not saying English should never change — I have no objection, for example, to 'skibidi' being added to the dictionary as it has been recently, despite how cringe the word is — but at the same time, when you change the meaning of an existing word away from its original semantical meaning, you are effectively making the word meaningless from an information theory standpoint, as you can no longer guarantee using the word will convey the information you need it to convey. 'Momentarily' semantically means 'for a moment', 'presently' semantically means 'in a moment', 'currently' semantically means 'at this moment'. Trust the words more than the dictionary, folks.

I'll be perfectly honest with you: the first time I watched this episode, I was fully expecting this setup to lead to a lesson on race relations. Isaac's being a African American reporter with that much access in 1951 by itself would have been rather remarkable, frankly — going through this episode more thoroughly now, even, the part about having no place to sit in the press box could even have underhandedly spoken to that, if only unintentionally. Isaac's aversion to talking on the matter quite easily could have been an artifact of something happening that day due to his being Black in "the White man's domain". That Mr. Sorkin didn't go there, to bring it back to my introduction, likely speaks to a certain amount of self-awareness that _Sports Night_ is first and foremost intended to be a half-hour comedy rather than a hour-long drama like _The West Wing_, where an exploration of the topic would have been more apropos. It does mean this subplot ends up falling a little flat, to a certain extent.

Anyway, this episode — we've hit a transition point in the season, and it's a welcome one. Dana finally gets the talk-down she deserves for her behavior, and Casey appears to have escaped from under the thumb of her behavior at the same time (as questionable as the escape's destination may be). The subplot involving Isaac did leave some possibilities on the table as previously mentioned, but it still holds water overall, if for no other reason than it gives Josh Charles an opportunity to fill Dan's character with an absolutely wonderful level of energy surrounding his appreciation for sports. How the season will continue from this point is an active question that viewers, myself included, are eager to see answered — on that front, the episode certainly did its job.
