Part of why I generally don't find much value in half-hour comedy shows is that many of them rely on having the character of the week practically lobotomized to drive the plot for that episode. _Sports Night_ certainly doesn't go full-tilt on that strategy like other half-hour comedies, but it was not immune from the strategy either — the treatment of Dan's character in the previous episode provides the most recent example. This episode, however, instead has everyone acting refreshingly human, while still managing to bring the funny demanded of the format. It is, of course, not the Emmy-winning Christmas episode of the year for Mr. Sorkin — but getting deep into that would be comparing apples and oranges, I would say. Let's keep our focus here and step through the episode.

Jeremy's romanticization aside, his anecdote about Secretariat's heart is... mostly true. While the necropsy did not actually weigh the late horse's heart, the head pathologist relayed qualitative astonishment at the "perfect" nature of said heart. When the same pathologist performed a necropsy for another thoroughbred who was a contemporary of Secretariat and found that horse's heart weighed in at 18 pounds, he estimated the weight of Secretariat's heart to be 22 pounds, which would have been two and a half times the weight of an average horse's heart. The theory that heart size contributes to the athleticism of a racing horse, however, has never definitively been proven.

So... yeah, this is certainly not the stronger of the two holiday episodes we get from this year of television. At the same time, though, it is still a solid half-hour of television, keeping true to the comedy format while still handling serious topics with some aplomb. Perhaps it would be more apropos to compare with the holiday episode of season one of _Sports Night_ — and in that comparison, I would rather counterintuitively say this episode stacks up well against last year's. While last year's episode has an undoubtably badass moment for Isaac, the episode overall was marred with tonal inconsistency and some nearly vomit-inducing lows for the character of Jeremy in particular. This episode doesn't swing for the fences in any capacity, but it keeps itself on a good level tonally — and arguably keeps the character of Isaac up on the pedestal established last season in a way that fits the current situation quite nicely. One almost wishes _Sports Night_ could have had an episode that brought in the best of both worlds for the holiday season, but that's perhaps once again too much to ask of a half-hour show.
