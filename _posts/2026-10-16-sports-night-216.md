I've yet to find a discernable pattern in the availability of draft scripts for Sorkin television episodes. Some scripts are observed nearly to the letter while others disclose massive changes between draft and episode, so the pattern can't be attributed to closeness to final product; likewise, the absolute highest-rated episodes of Sorkin television don't have draft scripts available, so popularity of the corresponding episodes doesn't dictate draft script availability. If anything, with a couple of exceptions the scripts usually exhibit how Mr. Sorkin tended to write too much for any given episode — like George Harrison writing song lyrics — but I find it hard to believe the available scripts represent the full picture in that regard. Whatever the case, we have the draft scripts we have, and this episode represents another opportunity to dig into the differences — and an "UPDATED FINAL" one to boot (for fuck's sake, Aaron). ### RETURNING Plot Bunny: Rely on the love of an audience
<sup>Previous instance: _Sports Night_ 205</sup>

> DAN: ... and you never heard of Bob Beamon.
> 
> ABBY: Not everybody needs the love of strangers.
> 
> DAN: He was an athlete, Abby. Athletes need to win.

For those who had to look it up like I did: Bob Beamon was the track and field athlete who held the long jump world record prior to the previously name-dropped Mike Powell. His tenure as the world record holder was nearly 23 years, though, so the inferable parallel to Oscar Parrish here is faulty. His inclusion here instead acts solely as a callback to Abby's being unable to name him previously — which makes its placement in the middle of this exchange a little suspect, to be perfectly honest.

> ABBY: You feel disconnected from people around you now?
> 
> DAN: Yeah.
> 
> ABBY: Yeah, people are challenging themselves, trying new things. Dana went to church, found out she liked it; Jeremy's on an adventure, you wonder why that didn't happen to you; meantime your partner got on the list and you didn't — just like Oscar Parrish.

So... that's supposed to be the common thread between all the subplots of this episode? The connection is tenuous at best, if you ask me. For one, Dan wasn't even in the room for Dana's tale of being lulled into the church-going experience; for two, Dan's reaction to Jeremy's "adventure" exhibited not nearly as much jealousy as Abby claimed it did, judicious dialogue cut notwithstanding. I suppose it's a strong enough connection for a doctor of psychology, a field which trains people to make the most of the human tendency to see patterns where there are none, but I'm not buying it myself.

> DAN: It was his whole life.
> 
> ABBY: Probably wasn't — and his whole life isn't over yet.

His whole life may not be over yet, but his _legs_? Did you hear how young the other guy was?

> ABBY: One of these days I'd like to go back to you sitting down.

Get your mind back out of the gutter, dear reader.

I'll be real with you, I don't know what to make of this episode. The one thought that sticks out in my mind the most is the suspicion that Mr. Sorkin was making the most he could out of what was at this point not his highest-priority project. We'll see the writing credits once again pull away from his direction as _Sports Night_ continues but not see the same for season one of _The West Wing_, which should tell you everything you need to know on that front. That isn't to say this episode is necessarily bad, mind you, just... a little all over the place, really — "unfocused" is perhaps the best word for it. It lends itself as a stepping stone for future episodes on a couple of fronts, but not much else.
