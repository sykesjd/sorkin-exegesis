I suppose a vociferous defender of _Sports Night_ would take my criticism of its season two plot thread indecision by offering that the same problem could be inferred from the first season of _The West Wing_ as well. On the surface they might have a point... but as I've alluded to in a previous entry, the multitude of plot threads is kind of the point with _The West Wing_: it better represents the necessarily multi-track nature of work in the White House (assuming functional occupants, of course). Additionally, the multitude of plot threads we get in _The West Wing_ never yields any thread that provides promise of a season-long arc then gets unceremoniously dropped like a hot rock mid-season, an issue _Sports Night_ experiences in spades. This episode in particular exhibits some of the strengths _The West Wing_ has over _Sports Night_ in that regard: a number of episode-length plot threads are introduced over a background of both a seasonal arc and what promises to be a show-length arc. To demonstrate:

This episode of _The West Wing_ has its fare share of questionable plot contrivances and continuity treatments, but that nonetheless does not detract from its status as a solid hour of television. In addition to providing another realistic view into the relative chaos of the White House, the window into the First Marriage receives a new layer of depth that establishes the First Lady as a political player of potentially equal stature to the man occupying the Oval Office. The continuing multi-episode drip feed concerning the consequences of Zoey and Charlie's relationship also gets built upon in a way that strongly promises to have further impact for the remainder of the season. I only wish Sam Seaborn would stop being painted as the incorrigible asshole he apparently is, but beggars can't be choosers, I suppose.
