I think it's fair to say, given the writing credit for this episode, that by this point Mr. Sorkin was seriously looking to hand off _Sports Night_ to its writers' room for subsequent seasons. We have here the fourth episode for which Mr. Sorkin eschewed writing credit, and in doing so a potential pattern emerges: Matt Tarses and Bill Wrubel were being trusted to take on the serious subjects, while David Walpert was being trusted to take on the threads of romance (or the fumbling thereof). Much like last time David Walpert ostensibly flew solo, I'd say the results are mixed — but that's arguably because if my aforementioned pattern matching is correct he overstepped his bounds slightly. I think it'll be obvious what I mean as we step through the episode.

### RETURNING Topical Signature: Soccer sucks
<sup>Previous instance: _Sports Night_ 103, 111, 201, 208</sup>

> CASEY: Don't go away, because when we come back, we'll take a look at one of nature's newest anomalies — a soccer game with high scores.

Those high scores cannot possibly be higher than the scores we just saw in this episode's game of Conflict Ball, I'm sorry.

I'll be real with you: this episode confuses me. The sudden-onset breakup of Natalie and Jeremy comes out of nowhere for me, and the sideshow of musketry with Dana and Sam feels... extra, and not in a good way. The subplot involving Steve Sarris wears out its welcome pretty quickly, which essentially leaves the episode to be little more than a long-form excuse for the cold open sequence from Josh Charles. As I stated at the top, Mr. Sorkin was likely preparing to hand off the show to the writers' room at this point, and while I will be extemporizing in a future entry how that was a wise move to consider, here the trust seems to have led the room off the rails a little. As I am not quick to point out frequently enough, though, that statement should be colored with the background that half-hour comedy generally isn't my cup of tea — laugh track or no, this episode errs on the side of that idiom more than previous episodes in this season, and to me it correlates neatly with an absence of Mr. Sorkin in the writing credits. Take from that what you will.
