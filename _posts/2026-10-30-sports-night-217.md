I closed out last entry noting how writing credits start drifting away from Mr. Sorkin toward the tail end of _Sports Night_, and exhibit A is upon us: we get the first of a two-parter, and yet Mr. Sorkin does not take sole credit for its writing. Never with _The West Wing_ did Mr. Sorkin not at least take sole credit on the writing of the teleplays for a two-parter. I rest my case.

Mind you, that doesn't necessarily mean this two-parter is doomed — on the contrary, that we've reached a proper two-parter in this show gives me some hope that perhaps the plot thread indecision that has plagued this season so far might finally make way for some plot thread clarity. We'll have to see for ourselves whether that hope is justified. As such, let's step through the episode.

As noted at the top, the plot thread indecision of this season of _Sports Night_ takes on a decidedly... decided turn of events in this episode, and I welcome it. In particular, the point of friction between Casey and Dan finally gets verbalized in a way that promises to give the rest of the season some room for exploration — room that was conspicuously not granted previously when friction was first established back in episode four of this season. While I'm still displeased with the treatment of Natalie's character, in the big scheme of things it manages to fit in well with the character arc Jeremy has been given for the remainder of this season through this episode. Admittedly, the latter does show its signs of not being 100% a Sorkin creation, as the writing credits will confirm, but as stated previously and will be stated again, Mr. Sorkin was rightfully looking to let the writers' room take over the show from him in the future. How the upcoming part two will fit into that scheme, of course, we'll have to reserve for next entry.
