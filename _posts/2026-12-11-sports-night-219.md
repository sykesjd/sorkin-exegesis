It has only occurred to me now that the latest story arc for Dan could theoretically be considered a character continuity error. Back in season one, the Christmas episode included a cold open in which Dan implored Casey to invest himself more into the show's being a team sport, a imploration which (after additional prompting from a certain guest spot) Casey heeds magnificently in the end. As it stands now, Dan had to be told off by Casey for clearly not wanting to invest himself in the show at all, a reversal almost as stark as the reversal in which of the two are more popular between the pilot and the rest of the show.

Shall we suppose that continuity error gets corrected in this episode? One way to find out: let's step through the episode.

By Joshua Malina's own admission, this Hebrew recitation is not an actual prayer, but something Jews use to seem Jewish on camera without sacrilegiously praying in vain — all because the tribal war god of Abraham has a hell of a vanity complex, as we all know.

And on that note... I made it through the episode. I honestly don't know what to make of this episode, to be frank — so it's probably just as well that Mr. Sorkin wasn't included in the writing credits. As a half-hour comedy show, it fits the formula of an "oh shit, we need to get things back to normal" episode rather religiously, so I'm naturally disinclined to take to the episode all that well. Nonetheless, re-establishing Dan's place as a man of the people brings his character back into the good graces of the audience at home, though it does briefly come at the cost of Casey's character herein. Almost certainly more could have been made of their post-fight dynamic, but the half-hour comedy format demands its toll, so we are set to move on. If only Natalie would move on as well...
