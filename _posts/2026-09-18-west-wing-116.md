Much energy has been spent on how the President-Vice President relationship in _The West Wing_ was at least in part modelled after the similar relationship between John F. Kennedy and his VP, Lyndon B. Johnson. It should be noted, however, that the two relationships differ in one crucial way: with _The West Wing_, the President is markedly older than the Vice President, rather than the other way around with JFK and LBJ. In point of fact, one could argue the dynamic should be considered reversed rather than directly modelled, as we hear later on how President Bartlet isn't the most popular guy in the Democratic party — words you'd be hard-pressed to hear of JFK. Both those points of difference get leaned into a little with this episode, to different extents — and to demonstrate:

> LEO: _(V.O.)_ Previously on _The West Wing_...

Let's step through the episode.

> BARTLET: I don't like putting him in this position.
> 
> LEO: I know.
> 
> BARTLET: I just didn't want you to think I was getting any pleasure from putting him in this position.

> BARTLET: ... it's ironic that it all comes down to a vote on the ethanol tax credit.
> 
> LEO: Yeah.

Okay, well, a well-read viewer can suss out basically everything they need to know from those two lines. Not all viewers are well-read, of course, so let's see how the drip feed continues.

For now, though, what shall we say about this episode? I suppose there's an argument to be made that this episode takes on a travel version of the "chaos of the White House" episode framework. Ethanol, gays in the military, flag burning, Zoey's everyday, Joey's apparent feelings for Josh — none of these truly get an A-plot treatment in this episode. The ethanol thread in particular leaves me wondering if drafted scenes got cut from the final product, considering how little we hear of the Vice President's argument before Leo changes his mind on the matter. The overall effect, however, is not to be denied: being President of the United States inherently makes one's life into chaos, whether at the White House or flying across the country. The additional touchstone of its effect on said president's family, while very lightly touched upon herein, promises to gain more charge as the season progresses. I only wish there were more lead-up into the apparent heel-face turn in Joey's regard for Josh, but I suppose asking Aaron Sorkin for coherent romance is asking too much.
