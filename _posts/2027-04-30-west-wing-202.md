Case in point!

As an origin story two-parter, the past two episodes provide plenty of surface-level exposition, but for the most part it's enough to get a good sense of what drove everyone to join the campaign. Toby finds promise in someone willing to tell the truth in the face of scrutiny; Josh finds promise in someone who actually openly stands for something; Sam finds promise in someone committed to a greater responsibility; and C.J. finds promise in someone actually worth marketing to the people. The only character whose introduction isn't quite on that level is Donna's, which while completely in-character for her doesn't have the same debilitatingly altruistic packaging of the others. Also, I suppose it should be noted the complete absence of Mandy is theoretically a little questionable — unless her presence in the campaign didn't come until after Illinois, I guess.

Aside from the origin stories, the two-parter has little to speak for beyond planting the seeds for episodes to come. Toby's somewhat half-baked storyline surrounding his guilt for indirectly getting the President shot seems to receive the most charge, though it gets wrapped up rather succinctly — as opposed to the plotline surrounding Danny's questions on the twenty-fifth, which seem to get hand-waved away by an off-screen conversation with Leo. I don't even know what to make of the necklace mini-plot, to be perfectly honest, other than presenting C.J. with an opportunity to show grace under fire, to mixed results. For a pair of episodes intended primarily as an origin story flashback, however, they do their job of enhancing the players' motivations serviceably, so I really have no point of complaint.
